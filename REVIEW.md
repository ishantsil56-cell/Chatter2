# IRIS — a review, by the developer

I built IRIS because I wanted a messenger that keeps conversations private without piling on features nobody asked for. It does the essentials well: one-to-one and group chats with text, photos, voice notes and files, plus polls, reactions, disappearing messages, message editing, and delete for everyone. Staying organised is easy too — pin, mute, star, mark unread, and search inside any conversation.

The part I care about most is the privacy design. Messages are end-to-end encrypted, and notifications are content-free, so the server never sees what you actually wrote.

It is deliberately not a WhatsApp clone. There are no voice or video calls, no Status and no channels — that is scope I chose to leave out, not polish I skipped. And it is an indie project distributed through GitHub rather than the Play Store, so installing it takes a little more effort than a store download. If you want a focused, private messenger without the bloat, IRIS is genuinely good at what it sets out to do.

— Ishant Sil
